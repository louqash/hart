What do CTL, ATL and TSB mean?
Fitness · CTL
Chronic Training Load. Your built-up fitness: a weighted average of daily training load over ~6 weeks (42-day time constant). It rises slowly with consistent training and fades slowly when you stop — the injury layoff shows as a long decline.
Fatigue · ATL
Acute Training Load. Recent fatigue: the same average over ~1 week (7-day time constant). It jumps after hard days and drops within a few rest days.
Form · TSB
Training Stress Balance = CTL − ATL. Above 0: fresh (tapered or resting). About 0 to −10: normal training. −10 to −30: productive overload. Below −30: heavy fatigue — injury and illness risk rises.
Ramp
How much CTL changed in the last 7 days. Steady small gains build fitness safely; big jumps are where overload injuries come from.

CTL and ATL are the same calculation on the same daily load — only the window differs (~6 weeks vs ~1 week). Form is the gap between them: negative when this week is harder than what you're used to, positive when you've been resting.

Daily load here is Garmin's training load (EPOC-based), not power/pace TSS, so the ranges above are rough guides.

Bike EF
Normalized power ÷ average heart rate — watts you produce per heartbeat. Rising EF at the same kind of ride means your aerobic engine is improving.
Run EF
Speed (metres per minute) ÷ average heart rate. Hills, heat, fatigue and intervals all lower it, so compare similar easy runs.

Indoor (◆) and outdoor (●) rides aren't directly comparable — trainer power and cooling differ. The line is the 28-day median.

{% endcall %} {%- endmacro %} {% macro decoupling_legend() -%} {% call info("What is aerobic decoupling?") %}

How much your efficiency drops from the first half of a steady session to the second half — heart rate drifting up while power or pace stays the same. Below 5%: your aerobic base holds for that duration. Above 5–10%: the session was longer or harder than your current endurance supports (or heat, dehydration, fatigue). Only steady sessions of 45 min or more are shown.

Your best average power for each duration — 5 seconds, 1 minute, 5, 20, 60 minutes and so on — across all rides in the period. The left side is sprint power, the middle (5–20 min) is VO2max and threshold, the right side (1 h+) is endurance, which matters most for an Ironman.
